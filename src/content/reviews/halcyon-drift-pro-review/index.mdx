Halcyon products are usually described as beautiful before they are described as
anything else, and the Drift Pro is no exception. What is unusual is that this time
the sound justifies the attention.

## The sound

Lifted low bass, forward presence region, and a treble that stays clean up to the
point most buds start to sizzle. It is a livelier tuning than the Atlas II and a
less honest one — vocals sit further forward than they were mixed to, and busy
recordings lose a little separation.

For the money that is a fair trade, and for the intended use — walking, commuting,
a gym — arguably the correct one. What impresses is the headroom: at high volume
there was no measurable compression and no audible distortion, which is not
something we can say about most of the $229 field.

Seven decibels behind a good over-ear at 100 Hz is roughly the physics of the format,
and no bud we have measured closes that gap. What the Drift Pro does do is get much
closer than most, which makes it a real alternative rather than a compromise.

## The case

We have thought about how to write this section without it sounding petty, and there
is no way, so: the lid opens in a bag.

The case is a machined aluminium lozenge, 58 mm across, with a magnetic hinge and no
detent. The magnet holds the lid closed against gravity when the case is upright. It
does not hold it closed when the case is on its side and something presses against
the seam, which is the state a case is in for the entire time it is in a bag.

Over five weeks we found the case open in a bag eleven times and lost a bud onto the
floor twice. Both were recovered. A third time would not have been.

<Callout tone="caution" title="This is a design defect, not a preference">
  We tested a second retail unit to rule out a faulty magnet. Same behaviour. If you buy these, keep
  them in a zipped pocket, and be aware that the case is the reason this review scores 7.6 rather
  than 8.4.
</Callout>

## Battery and everything else

Seven hours and ten minutes in the bud with cancellation on, measured at 75 dB SPL,
against a claimed eight. Twenty-nine hours with the case. Both figures are at the
low end of the current field and both are honest, which counts for something.

No wireless charging, which at $229 in 2026 is a genuine omission. The app is small,
fast, requires no account, and offers a five-band equaliser that actually works.

## The verdict

If Halcyon fixes the case, this is a 8.4 and an easy recommendation. As shipped, it
is a superb-sounding pair of earbuds that you have to look after, and the score
reflects both halves of that sentence.
