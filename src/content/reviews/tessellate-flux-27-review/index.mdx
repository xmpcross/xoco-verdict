distinguishes a good panel from an average one, which is whether the colour it shows
you is the colour in the file.

## Out of the box

We profile every monitor twice: once as it arrives in its default mode, and once
after a full calibration. The first number is the one that matters for almost
everybody, because almost nobody owns a colorimeter.

An average error of 1.3 is the sort of figure that used to require a monitor with a
factory calibration report and a four-figure price. Tessellate ships one in the box,
per-unit, and ours matched it.

<Callout tone="method" title="Why we quote out-of-box accuracy first">
  A monitor's calibrated performance tells you about the panel. Its out-of-box performance tells you
  what you will actually be looking at, because the overwhelming majority of monitors are never
  profiled. We publish both, in that order.
</Callout>

## Contrast, brightness, and the HDR claim

1780:1 measured contrast is good for IPS and nowhere near OLED. 428 nits full-field
is bright enough for a room with windows and not bright enough for direct sun.

The HDR certification on the box is the lowest tier available and means nothing at
this brightness. There is no local dimming, so enabling HDR raises the black level
across the whole panel and makes everything worse. Leave it off.

## The hub, which is the underrated part

96 W of power delivery, four USB ports, and 2.5 gigabit Ethernet, all live on both
the USB-C and the DisplayPort inputs — the second half of that sentence is the
unusual one. Plenty of monitors have a hub that only works when the video is coming
over USB-C.

With a Meridian 14 attached this is genuinely one cable: power, display, network,
keyboard, and an external drive.

## The menu

A four-way stick under the bottom bezel, six levels of hierarchy, and icons without
labels. Changing input takes five presses and we never learned the sequence. It is
the clearest possible illustration of where Tessellate spends money and where it
does not.

## The verdict

The best monitor under $700 we have measured, provided you never need to open the
menu. Turn HDR off, ignore the speakers, and enjoy a panel that is right the first
time you switch it on.
