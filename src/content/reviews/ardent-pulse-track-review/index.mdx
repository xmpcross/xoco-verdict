Almost every GPS watch is accurate enough on an open road. The differences appear
where the signal does not: between buildings, under trees, and on a track, where the
true distance is known to the centimetre and the watch has nowhere to hide.

## The track test

Twenty-five laps of a certified 400 m track in lane one, watch on the left wrist,
compared against the known distance. Then the same watch through a city loop with
tall buildings on both sides, and a wooded trail section under full canopy.

These are the best positioning numbers we have recorded, and not marginally. A 0.3%
distance error over a session means a marathon measured to within 130 metres, which
is inside the variation caused by your own running line.

## Heart rate, which is the other half of a running watch

Steady state is fine — within 2 bpm of a chest strap for the whole of a 45-minute
easy run. Intervals are not. On 400 m repeats the optical sensor takes about nine
seconds to catch a rise and about the same to catch the fall, which means the number
on your wrist describes the previous repetition.

<Callout tone="caution" title="Pair a chest strap if you train by heart rate">
  Nine seconds of lag is enough to make interval pacing by wrist heart rate actively misleading. The
  watch pairs with any ANT+ or Bluetooth strap and the accuracy problem disappears entirely, but
  that is another $60 and another thing to charge.
</Callout>

## The app

Ardent requires an account before the watch will finish setup. Activity history is
stored on Ardent's servers rather than on the phone; deleting the app removes local
access to everything. Exporting your own data in a standard format is part of a
$4.99 monthly tier.

We would score this harshly on any product. On a $399 one it is difficult to
justify, and it is the single reason we cannot recommend the Pulse Track to someone
who is not specifically buying it for the positioning.

## Battery and build

Twenty-six hours with multi-band GPS active, eleven days as a watch. Both are
mid-pack and both matched the claims. At 47 g with the supplied strap it disappears
on the wrist, and after seven weeks including one hard contact with a doorframe the
screen is unmarked.

## The verdict

A specialist instrument sold as a general-purpose watch. If you run where GPS
struggles, nothing else comes close. If you do not, you are paying $399 for a
chipset you will never notice and an app you will resent.
