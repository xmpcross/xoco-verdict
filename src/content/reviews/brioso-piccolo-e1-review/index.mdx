The difference between a machine that makes good espresso and a machine that makes
the same espresso twice is temperature stability, and it is the specification most
often quoted and least often measured.

## Temperature

We log brew water temperature with a thermocouple in a Scace device, pulling twelve
shots back to back at two-minute intervals after a full warm-up. What we are looking
for is not the absolute number — that can be dialled in — but the spread.

Four tenths of a degree over twelve shots is a result we would expect from a
dual-boiler machine at twice the money. It is the single best thing about the
Piccolo and the reason a beginner will improve faster on it than on almost anything
else at the price: when a shot tastes different, it is because something you did was
different.

<Callout tone="method" title="Why we measure at the group and not the boiler">
  Boiler temperature is easy to stabilise and tells you very little. What the coffee experiences is
  the water arriving at the puck, after it has passed through a group head that is a large lump of
  metal with its own thermal behaviour. Every espresso temperature figure on this site is measured
  at the group.
</Callout>

## The single-boiler problem

Fifty-five seconds. That is how long the machine takes to go from brew temperature
to steam temperature, and it is not optional — you pull the shot, press the switch,
wait, steam, then wait again for it to come back down before the next one.

For one flat white in the morning this is a mild inconvenience. For two people
wanting milk drinks at the same time it is the defining characteristic of the
machine, and no amount of temperature stability compensates.

## Living with it

The E1 assumes you already know things. There is no guidance on dose, no starting
grind recommendation, no explanation of why the first shot after warm-up will be
different, and the four-page manual spends two of those pages on descaling.

What you get instead is a machine that does not get in the way once you know what
you are doing: 58 mm standard everything, so any basket, any tamper, any distributor;
an OPV set correctly at the factory to 9 bar; and a parts diagram that means the
gasket you will need in three years is a $4 part rather than a service visit.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Two changes worth making immediately">
  Replace the pressurised basket with a standard one — the machine ships with the pressurised
  version fitted and it flatters bad grinding at the cost of everything else. Then set the PID to 93
  C and leave it alone for a fortnight.
</Callout>

## The verdict

If you are one person who drinks espresso and wants to get properly good at it, this
is the machine. If you are two people who drink cappuccinos, the wait will wear you
down long before the temperature stability wins you over.
