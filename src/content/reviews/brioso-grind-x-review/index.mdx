The grinder matters more than the machine. This is the least popular true statement
in home espresso, because grinders are expensive and boring and a machine looks like
something.

## Particle distribution

We sieve a 50 g sample through a five-stage stack and weigh each fraction. Fines —
the material below 100 micron — are what cause channelling and bitterness; a narrow
distribution with few fines is the entire reason to spend money on a grinder.

Seventy-one per cent of the mass falling within 100 micron of the peak is a very
good result, and it is audible in the cup: extractions are more even, and the range
of grind settings that produce a drinkable shot is noticeably wider. On a Piccolo E1
the pairing is excellent.

## Retention, and who it hurts

One point four grams stays in the chute after every dose. That coffee is not lost —
it comes out at the start of the next grind — but it means the first 1.4 g of your
morning espresso is yesterday's coffee.

If you drink one roast, week in and week out, this is invisible. If you buy 250 g
bags of different coffees and taste through them, it is a constant, small
contamination, and the only fix is to purge a few grams every time you change.

<Callout tone="method" title="How we measure retention">
  Grind 20 g through a clean, dry grinder and weigh the output. The difference is retention. Repeat
  three times with the same beans, then run a fourth dose of a visually distinct roast and weigh
  what comes out. Every retention figure on this site is measured this way.
</Callout>

Brioso does not ship a bellows or a blind tumbler, either of which would help. A
third-party bellows costs about $15 and drops retention to roughly 0.6 g in our
testing, which is the first thing we would buy alongside it.

<Callout tone="tip" title="If you single-dose">
  Add a bellows and weigh the output rather than the input. With one fitted, the Grind X becomes a
  genuinely good single-dosing grinder; without one, plan on purging 2 g every time you change
  beans.
</Callout>

## Everything else

Eleven seconds for an 18 g dose at 72 dBA — fast and loud, roughly in line with the
class. The stepless collar has about 90 degrees of usable range across espresso,
which is enough resolution to dial in without hunting.

Static is the one build complaint beyond retention: with light roasts in a dry
kitchen, grounds cling to everything. A drop of water on the beans before grinding
solves it entirely and is what we did for the last six weeks.

## The verdict

At $299 the burr performance is unmatched, and the retention is the price of it.
Budget another $15 for a bellows and this becomes very hard to argue with.
