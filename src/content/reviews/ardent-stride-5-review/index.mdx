Every running shoe is good for the first fifty miles. The interesting question is
what a midsole does over the following three hundred and fifty, and it is the
question most reviews cannot answer because most reviews are written after a week.

## What 400 miles does to it

We measure stack height at five points with a digital gauge before the first run and
again at 100, 250, and 400 miles, on two pairs run by two people of different weights.
Foam that packs out loses height, and losing height changes the geometry the shoe was
designed around.

Nine per cent of compression over 400 miles is the best figure we have recorded, and
the practical version of that number is that the shoe at mile 380 still feels like
the shoe at mile 80. Most trainers in this class go noticeably dead somewhere around
250, which is exactly the point at which people start saying a shoe "feels tired"
without knowing why.

<Callout tone="method" title="Why we run two pairs">
  Midsole compression is heavily dependent on body mass and footstrike. A single tester produces a
  number that describes that tester. Two pairs at 68 kg and 84 kg is still a small sample, but it
  catches the worst of it, and both figures are published in our wear-test log.
</Callout>

## The ride

Soft at easy pace and surprisingly willing when pushed. There is no plate, no
carbon, and no attempt at propulsion; what there is instead is a foam that
compresses a long way and comes back without the wallowing that usually accompanies
that much travel.

The 92 mm forefoot is the underrated feature. Wide platforms do the stability work
that medial posts used to do, without the shoe fighting a neutral runner. Over
eleven weeks neither tester reported the late-run instability that soft trainers
usually produce when form falls apart.

## Fit, and a warning

It runs short. Not slightly — consistently about half a size short of the Stride 4,
confirmed across both testers and on a Brannock. Ardent revises lasts more often
than it announces doing so, and if you are reordering by size from a previous pair,
you will get a shoe that does not fit.

The midfoot is also narrower than the forefoot width suggests. Wide feet will find
the front generous and the middle less so.

## Against the Tempo Carbon

Different jobs. The Tempo Carbon is 79 g lighter and measurably faster below 5:30
per kilometre, and it is finished at around 200 miles. The Stride 5 costs $105 less
and lasts twice as long. Most runners need one of these and think they need the
other.

## The verdict

The best daily trainer we have wear-tested, held back only by a sizing change nobody
announced. Try them on, go up half a size, and expect to be running in them next
spring.
