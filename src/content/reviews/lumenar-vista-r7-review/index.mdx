whose autofocus everyone works around. Photographers bought the system for the glass
and learned to pre-focus, back-button, and hope.

## The autofocus test

We shoot a fixed protocol: a subject moving unpredictably towards and across the
frame at between 8 and 20 km/h, 2,000 frames per body, same lens, same light, same
distance. A frame counts as a keeper if the intended eye is within the depth of
field at 100% magnification. It is a harsh test and it is the one that separates
marketing from behaviour.

Ninety-one per cent is not merely better than the R6, it is better than anything
else we have run this protocol against. The system also fails more gracefully: when
it misses, it misses by a small amount and recovers on the next frame, rather than
racking to infinity and coming back.

<Callout tone="method" title="Why the keeper rate and not the AF point count">
  A camera with 759 phase-detect points and poor prediction will lose to a camera with half as many
  and good prediction, every time. Point counts are a specification; keeper rate is an outcome. We
  only publish the outcome.
</Callout>

## Everything else

The sensor is a 33-megapixel update on a design first seen in 2023, and it performs
like one: 14.1 stops at base ISO, useful files to ISO 6400, and a highlight roll-off
that photographers coming from the R6 will recognise immediately. This is fine. It
is not the reason to buy the camera.

The body is unchanged in every way that matters, which after five weeks reads as a
feature. The grip is still deep enough for a gloved hand, the front and rear dials
are still where your fingers expect them, and the menus have not been reorganised
for the sake of it.

## The video caveat

4K/30 is unrestricted and good. 4K/60 shut down after six minutes and forty seconds
in a 26 C room, and after eleven minutes at 19 C. Lumenar does not publish a limit
and the camera gives no warning before it stops.

<Callout tone="caution" title="Do not buy this as a hybrid body">
  If video is more than an occasional secondary use, the overheat behaviour makes the R7 unreliable
  in exactly the conditions where you would want 60p. We have asked Lumenar whether a firmware
  change is planned and had no answer.
</Callout>

## Against the R6

If you photograph things that hold still, the R6 is 40% cheaper and produces files
you will struggle to distinguish. If you photograph things that move, the R7 is a
different camera, and the gap is the largest single-generation improvement we have
measured in this category.

## The verdict

A narrow, excellent upgrade. Buy it for the autofocus, keep your lenses, and shoot
video on something else.
