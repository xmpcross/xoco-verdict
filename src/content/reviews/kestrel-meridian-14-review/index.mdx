Laptop reviews are mostly written in the first ten minutes of a workload, which is
the only period in which thin machines look good. Run the same test for forty
minutes and the field separates into laptops that were engineered for a chart and
laptops that were engineered for a job.

## Sustained load is the whole review

The Meridian's processor is not remarkable. Its cooling is. Kestrel has spent the
three millimetres of extra thickness on a vapour chamber and two fans that spin up
early and slowly rather than late and hard, and the result is a clock curve that is
almost flat.

The Vector 13 wins the first sixty seconds of that test by a comfortable margin and
loses the remaining twenty-nine minutes by a much larger one. Which of those numbers
matters is entirely a question of what you do, and we would suggest that anyone who
exports video, compiles anything, or keeps forty browser tabs open is in the second
group whether they think so or not.

## Battery

Eleven hours and forty minutes at 150 nits with a mixed workload — browser, editor,
video call, some light compilation — across four runs that landed within eleven
minutes of each other. That is the most consistent battery result we have recorded,
and consistency matters more than the peak figure: a laptop that does 14 hours on a
good day and 6 on a bad one is a laptop you carry a charger for.

<Callout tone="method" title="What our mixed-use test contains">
  Forty per cent browsing on a fixed set of sites, twenty per cent text editing, twenty per cent
  video call, ten per cent compilation, ten per cent idle with the lid open. Screen fixed at 150
  nits measured, keyboard backlight off, Wi-Fi on, Bluetooth off. Run to shutdown, four times.
</Callout>

## The panel, and the one real compromise

512 nits, colour accurate enough out of the box for photo work, 120 Hz, and matte.
It is a very good IPS panel and it is an IPS panel. Put it next to the Vector 13's
OLED and the blacks look grey, because they are.

For most work this is the correct trade — IPS does not burn in, holds full-field
brightness better, and costs less battery. It is still the thing you will notice
first if you are coming from an OLED machine, and no amount of colour accuracy
argues you out of it.

## Repairability

Sixteen screws, all Torx T5, all the same length. Battery out in under two minutes.
Memory, storage, wireless card, fans, and speakers all socketed. The parts store
lists every one of them with a price, and the service manual is a PDF rather than a
support ticket.

This is not a small feature. A machine you can put a new battery into in 2029 is a
different purchase from one you cannot, and the five-year update commitment means
the software will still be there when you do.

## The verdict

Buy this if the work matters more than the bag. The Meridian 14 is not exciting,
does not photograph well, and will still be the fastest machine in the room at
four in the afternoon.
