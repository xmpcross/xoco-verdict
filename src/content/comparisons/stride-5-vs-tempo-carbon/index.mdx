## The economy test

Running economy is the amount of oxygen a runner uses at a fixed pace. A shoe that
improves it lets you run the same speed for less effort, and it is the only
laboratory measure that reliably predicts race performance.

We tested both shoes on a treadmill at three paces, on two runners, with a
randomised order and a repeat session a week later to check for a learning effect.

<Callout tone="method" title="How the economy testing worked">
  Two runners, three paces (6:30, 5:30, and 4:40 per kilometre), five minutes at each pace with the
  last two minutes averaged. Shoes randomised and the protocol repeated a week later. Reported
  figures are the mean of both sessions and both runners.
</Callout>

The result was clean and unsurprising: at 4:40 per kilometre the Tempo Carbon
improved economy by 2.1%. At 5:30 the improvement fell to 0.8%, which is inside our
measurement error. At 6:30 there was no difference at all, and both runners
independently described the plated shoe as "awkward" at that pace.

## Why the plate stops working

A curved carbon plate works with a rocker geometry that requires a certain amount of
force and a certain foot speed to engage. Below that threshold you are not being
propelled, you are running on a stiff, narrow, tall shoe, and the 84 mm forefoot
gives you less to balance on while you do it.

This is not a flaw in the Tempo Carbon. It is what a racing shoe is. The flaw is in
how these shoes are sold, which is as an upgrade rather than as a specialisation.

## The arithmetic

At $145 for 400 miles the Stride 5 costs 36 cents a mile. At $250 for 200 the Tempo
Carbon costs $1.25 a mile — three and a half times as much, for a benefit that only
exists in the fastest 10% of most people's running.

If you race four times a year, that is around 100 race miles including tune-ups, and
the shoe lasts two seasons. That is a defensible purchase. If you are buying it to
make Tuesday's easy run faster, it is not.

## Our answer

The Stride 5, comfortably, for almost everyone. The Tempo Carbon is a genuinely
excellent tool for a narrow job, and the mistake is buying it for a wide one.
