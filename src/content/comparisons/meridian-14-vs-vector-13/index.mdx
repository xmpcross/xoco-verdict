## One question

Does your work run for longer than a benchmark?

If it does — compiles, exports, renders, long calls with a lot of tabs — the
Meridian is the machine, because after eight minutes it is simply faster and it
stays faster. If it does not, the Vector wins on everything you touch: it is
lighter, it costs less, and its screen is better in a way you notice every day.

<Callout tone="method" title="The thirty-minute load test">
  Both machines ran an all-core load for thirty minutes at 22 C on a hard surface, with clocks,
  package temperature, and fan noise logged throughout. Neither was plugged into a dock or elevated.
  Reported figures are the mean of three runs with a full cooldown between them.
</Callout>

## What the Vector gives up, and when

For the first ninety seconds the Vector is the faster machine, by about 6%. At eight
minutes the two cross over. At thirty minutes the Meridian is 16% ahead and the
Vector's underside is 49 C.

None of that is a design failure. A 1.14 kg chassis has a fixed amount of thermal
mass and Tessellate has chosen to spend it on peak performance rather than sustained
performance, which is the right choice for the person who buys a 1.14 kg laptop.

The failure, if there is one, is that nothing on the Vector's specification sheet
tells you this.

## The screen is not a small thing

We have written elsewhere that the Meridian's IPS panel is very good, and it is: 512
nits, accurate, matte, and easy on a battery. Next to the Vector's OLED it looks
grey, because it is.

If you work in a dark room, edit photographs, or simply want the machine to look
expensive, the Vector wins this outright and there is no argument to make.

## The support window is the tiebreaker nobody checks

Kestrel commits to five years of BIOS and driver updates in writing, with published
changelogs. Tessellate publishes no commitment; its last three models stopped
receiving firmware around thirty months after launch.

On a three-year purchase that is irrelevant. On a six-year one it decides the
question, and it is the reason we would take the heavier machine.

## Our answer

The Meridian 14, on the strength of the thirty-minute number and the support window
— but the Vector 13 is the one we would recommend to someone who travels weekly and
replaces their laptop every three years.
