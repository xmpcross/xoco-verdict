## Same rig, same session

Both pairs were measured on the same head-and-torso simulator on the same afternoon,
with three runs each and the seal reseated between them. That matters more than it
sounds: isolation figures move by two or three decibels between sessions depending
on ambient temperature and how the pads or tips are seated, so numbers taken weeks
apart are not comparable.

<Callout tone="method" title="Why we re-measure for a comparison">
  Every figure in the table above comes from a single session with both products on the bench,
  rather than from the individual reviews. Where a comparison figure differs from a review figure,
  the comparison is the one to trust.
</Callout>

## The 7 dB

Seven decibels of additional isolation at 100 Hz is roughly the physics of the two
formats. An over-ear pair seals against the side of your head with a large,
compliant pad; an in-ear pair seals inside a canal with a small silicone tip. The
first has more surface area and more mass to work with, and no amount of processing
closes the gap.

What the Drift Pro does achieve is getting closer than most in-ears manage — 24.1 dB
is 4.7 dB better than the average of the ten other in-ears we have run through this
protocol. It is not a compromise product. It is a smaller product.

## Where the Drift Pro is genuinely better

Weight and presence. Over eleven hours of listening in a fortnight, both testers
reported taking the Atlas II off between tasks and leaving the Drift Pro in. That is
not a measurement, and it is the reason one of us bought a pair.

The tuning is also more immediately enjoyable. Whether that is a virtue depends
entirely on whether you want a recording reproduced or presented.

## The battery gap is bigger than it looks

Thirty-eight hours against seven hours ten minutes is a five-fold difference, but the
practical gap is larger, because the Drift Pro's charge cycle involves a case that
must itself be charged and that you must remember to close. Over five weeks we ran
the Drift Pro flat twice and the Atlas II never.

## Our answer

The Atlas II, unless the size is a constraint you cannot negotiate. Both are good;
one of them is measurably better at the thing they are both for.
