We spend a lot of words on chairs, and a chair is the right first purchase. But
the complaint we hear most often — a stiff neck and aching shoulders by mid
afternoon — is usually not a chair problem.

## The measurement

Sit normally. Look straight ahead. Your eyeline should land on the top quarter
of the screen, not the middle. For most people that means the top edge of the
monitor is level with or just below the eyes.

Then push the screen back until it is roughly an arm's length away, and tilt the
top away from you by about ten degrees.

## Why laptops are the worst case

A laptop screen is attached to the keyboard, so you can have the screen at the
right height or the keyboard at the right height, never both. There is no
posture that fixes this and no amount of sitting up straight that compensates.

A $25 stand and a $30 keyboard solve it completely, and that combination
outperforms every chair upgrade under $500 for this particular problem.

## The reflection nobody mentions

Once the screen is at the right height, check for a reflection in it. A window
or a symmetrical desk lamp behind you puts a bright patch in the middle of the
panel, and your eyes will fight it all day without you noticing. Move the lamp,
or get one with an asymmetric throw.
