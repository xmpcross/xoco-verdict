## The thirty-minute rule

If you read one thing on this page, read this. Laptop performance is almost always
reported from the first minute of a test, and the first minute is the minute in
which every machine looks the same.

Thin laptops handle heat by running fast until they cannot, then running slower.
Thick ones handle it by running slightly less fast for as long as you like. In a
ten-second benchmark the first design wins comfortably. In an export, a compile, or
a long video call with forty tabs open, it does not.

<Callout tone="method" title="What we did">
  Nine machines between $900 and $2,200, all bought at retail. Each ran a thirty-minute all-core
  load with clocks, temperature, and fan noise logged throughout, then our mixed-use battery
  protocol four times, then a colorimeter pass on the panel. Every machine was then used as a
  primary work laptop for at least a fortnight.
</Callout>

## Why not the fastest machine here

The Vector 13 posts the highest peak clock in this group and finishes a short
benchmark ahead of the Meridian. It is also 24% slower at the thirty-minute mark,
loud while getting there, and uncomfortably warm on the underside.

That is a real trade rather than a failure. If your work is a series of short bursts
between long idle periods — writing, email, browsing, spreadsheets — the Vector will
feel faster, weigh 380 g less, and have a better screen. We recommend it for exactly
that person.

## The thing nobody puts on a spec sheet

Support windows. Kestrel commits to five years of BIOS and driver updates in writing
and publishes changelogs. Tessellate publishes no commitment at all, and its last
three models stopped receiving firmware around thirty months after launch.

On a machine you keep for three years this does not matter much. On one you keep for
six, it is the difference between a laptop and a security problem. Ask before you
buy, and treat "we support our products" as a no.

## What to ignore when you shop

**Peak boost clocks.** A number the processor holds for a few seconds under ideal
cooling. It appears on every spec sheet and predicts nothing.

**"All-day battery."** Every manufacturer defines the day. Ask for the workload and
the screen brightness, or find a figure measured at a stated brightness.

**Port counts without protocol.** Four USB-C ports where two are 10 Gbps and cannot
drive a display is not four ports.

**Weight without thickness.** A 1.2 kg machine that is 18 mm thick fits in bags a
1.4 kg machine at 15 mm does not.
