Every few weeks someone writes in asking whether the open-back pair they are
eyeing really sounds as spacious as the reviews say. It does. That is also the
wrong question, and it is why the same pair is back on the second-hand market
three months later.

## What the grille actually does

An open-back earcup lets the driver breathe in both directions. Nothing is
reflected back at your ear, and the result is the airy, outside-your-head
presentation that reviewers reach for words like "soundstage" to describe.

The cost is that the earcup is, functionally, a small speaker pointed at the
room. Everyone near you hears a tinny version of your music, and you hear
everything they do. There is no isolation to speak of — not a little, none.

## The decision is about your room

If you listen at a desk in a room with a door you can close, open-back is a
genuine upgrade and you should not let anyone talk you out of it. A quiet room
is the one environment where its advantages exist and its disadvantage does
not.

Everywhere else — the train, the office, the sofa next to someone watching
television, the flat with a thin wall — the openness works against you. You
will turn the volume up to cover the room, the room will hear you do it, and
the spaciousness you paid for is buried under a compressor hum two desks away.

## Where that leaves the buying advice

Ask the questions in this order. Where will these be worn, most hours per
week? Does anyone else share that space? Only then: which one sounds better?

For most people the honest answers point closed-back, which is why every pair
in [our noise-cancelling guide](/post/best-noise-cancelling-headphones/) is
closed. That is not a judgement about sound quality. It is a judgement about
rooms, and the room wins.
