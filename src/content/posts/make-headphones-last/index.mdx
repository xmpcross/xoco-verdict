We keep every pair we test. Of the thirty-one still in the cupboard, eleven have
failed, and the failures are boringly consistent.

## The three failure points

**Earpads**, every time. Protein leather flakes at around two years of regular
use, and foam compresses long before that. This is the one that matters most for
performance: a compressed pad breaks the seal, and a broken seal costs more
isolation than the difference between a good cancellation system and a mediocre
one.

**The headband hinge**, on any pair that folds. It is a moving part under
tension carrying a cable through it.

**The cable entry**, on wired pairs, at the point where the cable leaves the
cup.

## What to do about it

Replace the pads on a schedule rather than on inspection. Eighteen months is
about right for daily use. Most pads cost between $20 and $40 and take ten
minutes with no tools.

Before you buy a pair, search the manufacturer's site for a parts page. If pads
and a headband cushion are listed with prices, the pair has a five-year life. If
they are not, it has a two-year life, and you should price it accordingly.
