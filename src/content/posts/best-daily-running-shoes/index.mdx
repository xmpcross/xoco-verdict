## Why 400 miles

Almost every running shoe review is written after a week. A week is fifty miles, and
at fifty miles every trainer in this test felt good.

The interesting question is what happens between mile 150 and mile 400, because that
is where a shoe either keeps the geometry it was designed with or quietly turns into
something flatter, softer, and less stable. Runners experience that as a shoe
"feeling tired" without knowing why, usually somewhere around the point they stop
enjoying their easy runs.

<Callout tone="method" title="What we did">
  Twelve trainers between $120 and $180, all bought at retail, two pairs of each run by testers at
  68 kg and 84 kg. Stack height measured at five points with a digital gauge at 0, 100, 250, and 400
  miles. Energy return measured fresh and at the end. Every pair run on the same mix of road and
  light trail.
</Callout>

## One shoe or two?

Most runners need one good trainer. The rotation advice you read online mostly comes
from people who have shoes sent to them.

That said, the case for a second pair is real if you race. A plated shoe gives back a
measurable amount at pace — 2.1% in our economy testing — and gives back nothing at
all below about 5:30 per kilometre, where the plate does not engage and you are
running on a narrow, unstable, expensive trainer. If you race four times a year, the
Tempo Carbon is worth it. If you do not, it is not.

## What to ignore when you shop

**Stack height alone.** A 40 mm stack in a narrow shoe is less stable than a 34 mm
stack on a 92 mm platform. The width is the number nobody prints.

**Weight, for a daily trainer.** Twenty grams is a rounding error over an easy hour.
It matters for racing and almost nowhere else.

**Foam names.** Every manufacturer has a proprietary blend with a capital letter.
The only thing that separates them in our testing is how much of the stack survives
400 miles, and that is not on the box.

**Your size from the last model.** The single most common source of a bad purchase.
Ardent moved the Stride 5 half a size, and did not say so anywhere.
