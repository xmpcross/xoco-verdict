The most common message we get about espresso is some version of "my shots are
sour and I think I need a better machine". Usually the machine is fine.

## Weigh both ends

A scale that reads to 0.1 g is the single highest-return purchase in home
espresso, and it costs about $25. Weigh the dry coffee going in and the liquid
coming out. Until you do that, every adjustment you make is being measured
against a number you do not know.

Start at 18 g in, 36 g out, in about 28 seconds. That is not a rule, it is a
starting point you can move away from deliberately.

## Wait for the machine

Every machine we have measured takes at least twelve minutes from cold to a
stable brew temperature, and several take twenty. The first shot pulled at
minute five will taste different from every shot after it.

## Distribute before you tamp

Most channelling — water finding a path through the puck and running out early —
starts as an uneven bed of grounds, not as an uneven tamp. Stir the grounds in
the basket with a thin tool before tamping. It takes four seconds.

## Change one thing at a time

Grind, dose, and time all interact. Change one, pull two shots, taste. Change
two at once and you have learned nothing.
