When we measure earbuds on the rig, the difference between a sealed tip and a
slightly leaking one is 8 to 12 dB below 200 Hz. To put that number in
context: no earbud we have ever tested differs from its rivals by that much.
The gap between the best and worst pair in a category is smaller than the gap
between the right tip and the wrong one on the same pair.

## The thirty-second test

Play something with a steady bassline. Press each bud gently into your ear and
hold it there. If the bass suddenly fills in while you press, the tip is not
sealing on its own — it is the wrong size or the wrong depth, and everything
you have concluded about how these earbuds sound is a review of the leak.

## How to actually fit them

Start one size up from whatever came fitted. A tip that seals should feel
present but not stretched, and it should survive the yawn test: open your jaw
wide, and the seal holds.

Fit each ear on its own. Around a third of the people we have sat down with
ended up in different sizes left and right, and every one of them had been
wearing matched mediums out of the box.

Depth matters as much as diameter. A tip lodged at the entrance of the canal
with the bud hanging off it will leak no matter the size; twist it in so the
bud sits flush and stays put when you shake your head.

## When to spend actual money

If none of the bundled sizes seal, third-party tips in half sizes and foam
cost a few dollars and rescue otherwise excellent earbuds. That is the entire
upgrade path. It is not glamorous, but it is 10 dB, and nothing else in audio
sells 10 dB for pocket change.
