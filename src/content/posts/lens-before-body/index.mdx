Camera marketing is organised around bodies, because bodies are what get
replaced. The decision that actually determines what your photographs look like
in ten years is which mount you buy into, and that decision is about glass.

## The asymmetry

A sensor generation is about three years and buys you a stop of high-ISO
performance and better autofocus. Real, but incremental.

A good lens design stays in the catalogue for a decade or more and does not get
meaningfully better, because the physics has not moved. The 35mm prime we
recommend today is optically the same lens it was in 2023.

## What this means when you buy

Work out which lenses you want before you decide which body to buy. Look at the
whole catalogue for a mount, including the used market — that is where a mount's
twenty-year history pays out.

Then buy the least expensive body that has the autofocus you need. Put the
difference into glass.

## The one exception

If your photographs are failing for a reason that is a body problem — you cannot
track moving subjects, or you need more than about six stops of dynamic range in
one frame — then it is a body problem, and a lens will not fix it. That is
narrow, and it is not most people.
