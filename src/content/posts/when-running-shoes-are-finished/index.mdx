The tread on a running shoe is the last thing to go and the first thing people
look at. By the time the outsole is worn through, the midsole has usually been
dead for a hundred miles.

## The thumb test

Press hard into the midsole foam just behind the ball of the foot, hold for two
seconds, and let go. A fresh shoe pushes back and recovers immediately. A
finished shoe feels dense, dents, and recovers slowly or not at all.

Compare against a new pair of the same model if you can. The difference is
obvious once you have felt both.

## The compression numbers

We measure stack height at five points before and after a 400-mile wear test.
Across nine cushioned trainers the average loss was 22%; the best we have
measured lost 9%. Somewhere around 15% is where both of our testers
independently start describing a shoe as flat.

## What that means in miles

For most cushioned trainers, 300 to 450 miles. Lighter runners get more, heavier
runners get less, and plated racing shoes get far less — around 200.

## The cheap insurance

If you run more than about 25 miles a week, rotate two pairs. Foam recovers
between runs, and two pairs alternated will together outlast the same two pairs
run into the ground one after the other.
